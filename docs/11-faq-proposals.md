## The three that matter most

These are not the obvious ones. They are the ones with real search volume, low competition, and a
direct bearing on whether an informed funder trusts SKO.

### 1. ✅ Does SKO run an orphanage or residential care centre?

> **No.** SKO does the opposite: our work is to prevent children being separated from their families
> in the first place. We provide counselling, case management and practical support so that families
> under pressure can stay together. Cambodia has learned at considerable cost that children do better
> in families than in institutions, and every part of the Family Development Program is built on that
> principle.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** Cambodia's orphanage sector has a well-documented history of
children being placed in residential care who had living parents. Funders who know the country ask
this question early, and often silently. Answering it plainly — and explaining that preventing
separation *is* the programme — separates SKO from organisations that still fundraise on
institutional care. It is also a question people ask AI assistants directly.

### 2. ⚠️ Can I visit SKO's projects or meet the children?

> Our programmes are delivered in families' own homes, and we do not arrange visits to meet children.
> This is a safeguarding requirement, not a matter of convenience: children are not an attraction, and
> repeated contact with visitors is harmful to children who have already experienced instability.
> Partners and funders are welcome to meet our team and discuss the work — contact us at
> info@samatapheapkhnom.org.

**Needs confirming:** whether SKO ever hosts funder or partner site visits, and under what conditions.
The safeguarding position above is the sector standard, but the exact policy is SKO's to state.

This is a high-volume search topic in Cambodia. Answering it responsibly positions SKO as the expert
rather than the exception.

### 3. ✅ Is SKO a religious organisation?

> No. SKO is a local, neutral, non-sectarian, non-political, non-racial and non-profit organisation,
> registered with the Cambodian Ministry of Interior. Support is offered to families on the basis of
> need alone, with no religious or political condition attached.

Straight from SKO's own registration language, and a question institutional funders ask as a matter of
routine due diligence.

## Our work — Family Development Program

| | Question | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | **What is the Family Development Approach?** — *A structured method for working with families in poverty, built on the premise that families are not short of capability but of support, information and access. Social workers visit families at home, assess the situation together, agree objectives the family sets for itself, and work through them over a series of visits.* | ✅ |
| 9 | **How long does SKO work with a family?** — *There is no fixed length. Work begins with an initial visit and a full family assessment, followed by home visits every week or twice a month. When the family's objectives are met, the case moves to phase-out — and SKO returns six months later to re-assess.* | ✅ |
| 10 | **What happens after SKO stops working with a family?** — *SKO re-assesses every family six months after phase-out. That follow-up is the point: it is the difference between a case that was closed and a family whose situation actually held.* | ✅ |
| 11 | **Why does SKO work in people's homes rather than at a centre?** — *Because the obstacles are in the home. Visiting families where they live means seeing the actual conditions, and it removes the cost and time of travelling to an office — which for a family already stretched is often the reason support never starts.* | ✅ |

## My recommendation

**Publish the nine marked ✅ now.** They are answerable entirely from material SKO has already
written, and every one of them is a question a real person asks.

**Prioritise questions 1, 2 and 15.** The first two are where SKO can own search and AI answers in a
space larger organisations handle badly, and the third is the one where getting it wrong has a cost
that has nothing to do with marketing.

**Nine questions is about the ceiling per page.** Beyond that they stop being read and start being
skimmed, and structured data rewards a few good answers over a long list.

One caution: every answer above is drafted in SKO's voice from SKO's material, but the ⚠️ ones state
positions the organisation has to actually hold. Do not publish a safeguarding position SKO has not
agreed internally — an answer engine will quote it back for years.
