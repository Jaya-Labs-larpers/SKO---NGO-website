01 — DIRECTION

White canvas, slate blue as the only voice of authority

White isn't a background here — it's the material. Structure comes from hairline rules and generous air, not from grey boxes or drop shadows. #475D8C is rationed: primary buttons, a short accent rule, the impact numerals, link underlines, the footer. Nothing else competes for it. Your two light blues — #C3E9E8 and #9BE1FF — do the softening: chips, the decorative quotation mark, the hero wash, the allocation bars, the focus glow. They're surfaces, never text. The result reads institutional rather than campaign-y, which is what a grant officer needs to see in the first three seconds.

The four moves
Air over ornament

72px section rhythm, 44px card padding. Emptiness is the design.

Lines, not shadows

1px #E0E6F0 borders define every surface. Shadow appears only on hover and in overlays.

Numbers carry the trust

Impact figures are set at 46px in blue with a context line — the largest type on the page after the H1.

Khmer is not an afterthought

Its own family, leading and size, enforced in the base layer by :lang(km).

All copy below is [PLACEHOLDER]. Khmer strings are structurally correct but must be written and reviewed by an SKO staff member before launch — see the Phase 1 risk register. No SKO facts, figures, staff names, or a director's name have been invented.

Amber is gone — the supporting tints do that work now. These two exist purely to mean something and are never decorative. Red also carries "closes in N days" on job posts, which is semantically right and adds no new hue. The success green appears in one state only (a sent form) — say the word and I'll switch it to blue with a tick.

Rules that survive contact with a CMS
  • Blue fills exist in exactly two large areas per page, maximum: one CTA band and the footer.
  • No colour is ever the sole carrier of meaning — every chip pairs colour with a word.
  • Body text is never lighter than ink-400. There is no lighter grey available to reach for.
  • Photos never sit under text without a white card between them (see the home hero).

04 — SPACE, RADIUS, ELEVATION, MOTION

The quiet tokens

Radius
xs 4
sm 6
md 8 · buttons, inputs
lg 12 · cards
xl 16 · panels

No pills except the language toggle and pagination. Fully rounded corners read consumer-app; this site needs to read institutional.

Elevation — deliberately weak
xs
sm
md · hover
lg · hero card

On a white site a shadow reads as haze. Cards ship with a 1px border and no shadow; the shadow is the hover state. Shadows are tinted with #111A2B, not black — a neutral-black shadow on a warm-white page looks dirty.

Rhythm & motion
TokenValueApplied to
--spacing-sectionclamp(3.5rem, 2rem + 6vw, 7rem)Vertical padding of every page section
--spacing-gutterclamp(1.25rem, .75rem + 2vw, 2.5rem)Page side padding — 20px phone, 40px desktop
--container-page1200pxMax content width
--duration-fast140ms cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)Hover, focus, colour changes
--duration-base220msMobile nav, lightbox, disclosure

No scroll-triggered reveals, no parallax, and no count-up animation on the impact numbers. A figure that animates on scroll reads as marketing; a figure that is simply there when the section arrives reads as a fact. That distinction is the whole job of this site. Everything above collapses to 0.01ms under prefers-reduced-motion.

Why the text version ships too

An image of a chart is invisible to a screen reader and unreadable at 360px no matter how good the zoom is. The image is the primary treatment you asked for — click or Enter opens a native <dialog> with the full-resolution file, pinch-zoom on touch, Esc to close, focus returned to the trigger. The collapsible text version underneath is one extra CMS field and it's what makes the page actually AA.

The alt field in Sanity carries a description that spells out the reporting lines in words, with the field help text telling staff exactly that.

This is one of the two treatments you'd already agreed. I've kept the image-first design and added the text fallback beside it rather than instead of it.

The language toggle — exactly what it does
  • It does not translate. It loads the same page from the Khmer field the staff member typed. There is no machine translation anywhere in this build.
  • One document, one Publish. English and Khmer can't drift out of sync or get published separately by accident.
  • If the Khmer field is left empty, that block falls back to the English text with lang="en" on it — so the page still works and a screen reader still switches voice correctly. The Studio shows a warning on save, but never blocks publishing.
  • The toggle always links to the same page in the other language, never back to the homepage.

If you'd ever want a "draft the Khmer for me" button in the Studio that staff then review before publishing, that's buildable — but it's a Phase 4 add-on, it needs an API key, and for a child-protection NGO I'd want a human sign-off step in it. Not in scope unless you ask.
